We’re obsessed with feeding LLM’s perfect data, whether that is synthetic data, curated data, RLHF-aligned data. But are we creating models that can’t handle the messy reality they’ll face in production?

Think about a child who was born into an upper class family. The child’s parents are terrified that they will raise their child to be a bad kid. To combat this, they implement some rules. The child is not allowed to leave the house until they are of a certain age and maturity level. The child will be homeschooled with content hand picked by the parents in order to help the child be the best person they can be. 

Now that same child gets to an age and maturity level, and it’s time for them to fly the coop. Because the child has ingested all this great curated content, the child kills all their job interviews, and gets a great job in a big city. 

The first day on their job, their manager and a senior colleague give them conflicting instructions on the same task. The young adult freezes. They’ve never had to navigate ambiguity or weigh competing perspectives. Unable to make a judgement call, they ask a third coworker for help. This third coworker, recognizing their naivety, manipulates them into doing their work instead. The young adult would comply immediately because they have no framework for recognizing bad-faith actors, or social manipulation.

Our LLMs face the same gauntlet. Contradictory prompts that expose their inability to handle ambiguity, followed by adversarial attacks that exploit their lack of exposure to manipulate patterns.

Now someone might be thinking, well that young adult, or our LLMs, need more synthetic data, better RLHD, or stricter guardrails. But that’s just doubling down on the sheltered approach. It’s like the parents responding to their child’s failure by thinking “we should have controlled the environment more”.

I see it as a 2 step approach. First, train on messy, diverse reality data. Then, build character on top of that.

First, let the models encounter contradictory information, adversarial patterns, edge cases, ambiguity, etc. And not as anomalies to be filtered out, but as core parts of their foundation. The model learns that the world is messy.

Then, we build character on top. Here the key - I am not talking about typical alignment, like feature clamping, rule-following and guardrails. I’m talking about core values. Integrity. Intellectual honesty. Fairness. Compassion. Responsibility. 

You cannot truly understand, let alone practice, those values without understanding the complexities of the world. A child who’s never witnessed dishonestly can’t develop integrity; they’re just compliant. A model that’s never seen manipulation can’t develop principled resistance; it’s just filtered.

Real character emerges from navigating difficult trade offs, recognizing context, and choosing the right action despite complexity.
